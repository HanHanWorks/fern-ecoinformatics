# 12. Data and Code Availability

The benchmark is released as metadata plus code, and the openly licensed part of it as images as well. The package contains: the manifest of the 5,969-image balanced benchmark (`manifest_balanced.csv`, 31 fields per image, including image domain, source, country, taxonomy, licence, rights holder, source identifiers and URLs, and the split group key) and of the 6,237-image pre-balancing pool; the 4,185/882/902 splits and the seven evaluation subsets of Sections 5 and 6; the 700-image open-set negative manifest; the 1,432 (24.0%) openly licensed images as a single archive with a per-image licence file; and all code under an open-source licence, which rebuilds the manifests, trains and evaluates every model reported here, and regenerates Tables 1–10 and Figures 3–8, with per-run configurations, metrics and per-image predictions for the 37 experimental runs. The repository documentation describes each artefact, the licence layers and the reproduction environment in full.

Every image carries the licence recorded by its source platform: CC0, public domain, CC BY and CC BY-SA are classed as redistributable, and the two NonCommercial variants — the only reason any image is withheld — account for all 4,537 restricted records. The code is released under the MIT licence, the manifests and derived metadata under CC BY 4.0; no image is relicensed.

The open subset is not a random 24% of the benchmark, and the skew limits what a reader can reproduce from the images alone. The released images are 63.3% herbarium specimens, against 28.0% in the benchmark as a whole, because institutional digitisation releases sheets under permissive terms (54.3% of GBIF specimen images) while volunteer field photographs are predominantly CC BY-NC (10.2% of iNaturalist images). Only 216 of the 902 test images (23.9%) can be redistributed — 69 of 640 field images against 147 of 262 specimen images — so a reader with the open archive can reproduce the specimen-side results and the specimen-to-field direction of Section 5, but not the field-side experiments or the geographic holdouts. The open archive should not be used as a stand-alone field-identification benchmark, and accuracies recomputed on it will not match the numbers in this paper.

Three details affect exact reproduction and are recorded in the released configuration files. The label space is rebuilt from the filtered training split, a correction disclosed in Section 5.4. Seed standard deviations are population standard deviations (ddof = 0). Fine-tuning is not bit-deterministic (about ±0.2 pp per re-run), which is why the recipe comparison is reported as a three-seed mean; the frozen probe reproduces exactly.

## Data availability statement

> The image manifests, split files, evaluation subsets and open-set negative manifest, the source code, the per-run configurations and metrics, and the per-image predictions are openly available at [repository URL]. Of the 5,969 images in the benchmark, 1,432 (24.0%) are published under their original Creative Commons licences, together with a per-image licence and attribution file. The remaining 4,537 images are licensed CC BY-NC or CC BY-NC-SA and are therefore not redistributed. They are fully described in the manifest, which records the licence, rights holder, source record identifier and source URL for each image. The dataset can be reconstructed from iNaturalist and GBIF subject to their terms. The openly licensed subset covers 216 of the 902 test images and over-represents herbarium material relative to the benchmark as a whole. The limits this places on independent reproduction are set out in Section 12. Archived versions of the manifests and code are deposited at [DOI].
